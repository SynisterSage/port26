# AI + Recruiter Screening Criteria for Portfolio Review

## Scope Note
This document reflects common screening patterns used across large hiring organizations and recruiting tech stacks.
It does **not** claim access to proprietary internal ranking formulas from any single company.

## How Screening Actually Happens Now
Most hiring pipelines combine:
- Recruiter skim (fast human filter)
- Hiring manager skim (quality and ownership filter)
- ATS/profile parsing (resume and keyword consistency)
- AI-assisted sourcing or ranking signals (fit, evidence, consistency, credibility)

Your portfolio must pass all four layers.

## 1) Role Match Precision
- Exact role fit in headline/title (`Product Designer`, `UI/UX Designer`, etc.).
- Domain fit when relevant (consumer, SaaS, mobile, fintech, healthcare).
- Seniority clarity (new grad, junior, mid-level).

### Strong Signal
- One primary role target with optional secondary lane.

### Weak Signal
- Vague titles like "creative" without role specificity.

## 2) Evidence Density
- Tangible outcomes (adoption, conversion, retention, task completion, support reduction).
- Scope signal (team size, timeline, responsibility boundaries).
- Shipping proof (live links, app store listing, production examples).

### Minimum Standard
- 2 measurable outcomes in each flagship project where available.

## 3) Case Study Structure Quality
High-performing case studies expose:
- Problem
- Constraints
- Role
- Process
- Decisions
- Tradeoffs
- Outcome

If this structure is missing, reviewers often classify work as visual-only.

## 4) Decision-Making Signal
- Why a decision was made, not just what was made.
- Before/after rationale.
- Usability, business, and technical constraints balanced together.

## 5) Keyword and Vocabulary Alignment
Systems and reviewers look for language found in job descriptions:
- User research
- Information architecture
- User flows
- Wireframes
- Prototyping
- Usability testing
- Design systems
- Accessibility
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Handoff and iteration

## 6) Consistency Across Surfaces
- Portfolio, resume, and LinkedIn should tell the same story.
- Dates, titles, and tools should not conflict.
- Work history should be easy to reconcile.

## 7) Readability and Scan Speed
- Fast scannability on desktop and mobile.
- Clear hierarchy and concise copy blocks.
- Important proof near the top of each project page.

## 8) Professional Risk Flags (Negative Signals)
- Too many unrelated or low-signal projects on the main page.
- Missing role ownership (unclear what candidate actually did).
- No measurable outcomes anywhere.
- Broken links or unfinished sections.
- Overly academic framing for industry-targeted roles.

## 9) Portfolio Conversion Signals
- Clear contact path.
- Resume available in one click.
- Stated availability and role target.
- Confidence-building social proof (clients, publication, shipped usage, testimonials).

## 10) Practical Pass Standard for Selective Product/UI-UX Roles
A portfolio is usually competitive when:
- Role match is immediate.
- 3-5 strong case studies are deeply documented.
- At least 2-3 projects show real outcomes with measurable impact.
- Candidate ownership is explicit and credible.
- Messaging is consistent across portfolio/resume/LinkedIn.

## Weighted Scoring Model (For Internal Use)
Score each category from 0-10:
- Role match clarity: 20%
- Case-study depth: 25%
- Evidence/metrics: 20%
- ATS/keyword alignment: 15%
- Consistency across surfaces: 10%
- Conversion readiness: 10%

Interpretation:
- 8.5-10.0: selective-role ready
- 7.5-8.4: competitive, needs polishing
- 6.5-7.4: good baseline, proof gaps remain
- <6.5: positioning risk

## Quick Self-Audit Checklist
- Can a recruiter identify role fit in 5 seconds?
- Can a hiring manager understand one case study in 90 seconds?
- Are at least 2 outcomes quantified in each flagship project?
- Do portfolio and resume use the same role language?
- Is there any project on the homepage that weakens role fit?

## Final Reality Check
Following this framework should significantly improve your score and interview odds, but no framework can guarantee offers.
Offers still depend on market timing, role competition, referrals, interview performance, and portfolio-to-job match quality.
